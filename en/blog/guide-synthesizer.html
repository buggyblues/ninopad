Getting Started with Synthesizers: Play beautiful chords and add drum beats for inspiration

Easily start with seventh-level chord keys, master sustain skills, enable tone editing, generate rich drum beat variations, and control playback at any time, giving you a comprehensive understanding of the four major functional desktops of the synthesizer.

September 11, 2026

There’s no need to cumbersomely connect a Mac or have a deep knowledge of arranging. Turn on the NinoPad synthesizer and tap a chord key to instantly play a beautiful melody. This tutorial will take you from listening to single-note chords to seamlessly switching to the arranger desktop to add drum beats. All steps can be listened to and debugged on the mobile phone in real time.

Picture description:The pictures are from the native app in the dedicated simulator, and are actually operated and screenshotted by the script. The connection state uses demo data; the local operation in this article does not require a Mac connection. Screenshots do not serve as verification of actual Mac MIDI output.

Before starting

  • Update to Milkcat Magic Control 1.3.0, turn on the sound and start practicing at a lower volume.
  • Select "Synthesizer" from the "Layout" at the bottom and enter the "Chord" desktop first.
  • This exercise uses the local high-quality sound source of the mobile phone; the Mac MIDI output can be configured as needed in subsequent connections.

1. Press a chord key first

There are seven numbered chord keys clearly arranged on the chord desktop. The upper row is 2, 4, and 6, and the lower row is 1, 3, 5, and 7. Press and hold the 1 key first and then release it to listen to a clear and full attack and decay; then click the 4 or 5 keys in sequence to compare the auditory colors of different chord progressions.

The small screen above displays the current key, chord names and performance status in real time. The numbers represent the steps in that key (for example, step 1 is not limited to C major). When you first start playing, it is recommended to keep the current key and focus on switching between different key positions. This will make it easier to establish an auditory perception of the changes in the series.

2. Hold a chord with sustain

Click the Sustain switch and press a chord key. Release your fingers at this time, and the chord sound will still continue melodiously, allowing you to free up your hands to adjust other knobs or switch desktops; the current picture shows the performance state after the sustain is turned on.

After practice, click "Sustain" again to turn it off. If the sound continues, check to see if arranger or loop playback is also enabled. "Sustain" mainly controls the note continuation of a single chord, and the play key is responsible for scheduling the overall project rhythm.

3. Click the small screen to view the current performance settings

Click on the small screen above to bring up the detailed editing panel. The parameters in the panel will be dynamically adjusted according to the current desktop and performance mode; it is recommended to first observe the parameter partitions on the interface, fine-tune one knob at a time, and then return to the desktop to strike the same chord for listening comparison.

After adjusting, click "Done" in the upper right corner to return. If you need to deeply customize the synthesizer tone, you can switch to the "Tone" desktop. Avoid making large-scale changes to multiple parameters at the same time before remembering the original sound, so that you can clearly feel the tuning effect of each knob.

4. Switch to the arrangement and generate a drum beat

Swipe the desktop switch bar at the bottom of the app to enter the "Arranger" desktop, or directly select it from the layout list. The four synthesizer desktops of chords, sounds, arrangements, and loops belong to the same project, so there is no need to recreate files.

Click the "Variation" button to randomly generate a layered drum rhythm, and then click "Play". The step indicator light will jump with the beat, and the play button will switch to the stop state synchronously. It is recommended to listen to one or two measures in full to confirm the rhythm and charm before making adjustments to avoid confusion among multiple variations caused by rapid continuous taps.

5. Open the arranger editor and get to know different parts

During the playback process, you can observe the rhythm in real time; before in-depth fine-tuning, click Pause, then click the small screen above the arranger desktop to call out the editing page. The arranger editor divides the music content into independent pages for drum beats, chords, bass, melody and whole song structure. It is recommended to familiarize yourself with the percussion points of each step from the drum beats page.

What is adjusted here is the voice sequence within the project. If you just want to change the sound quality of the instrument, it is more direct to go to the "Tone" desktop; if you need to record improvisation, you can go to the "Loop" desktop. First adjust one drum beat smoothly, and then gradually add other parts.

6. Stop this round of practice

Click "Finish" to return to the main desktop, confirm that the play button has returned to the waiting state, and turn off the sustain smoothly. This can clearly distinguish the actual effects of single note duration, timbre change and rhythm loop.

Next, you can also explore the real-time recording, overdubbing and undoing functions of the loop desktop, or save and export the current project as a WAV audio or MIDI file for more operations. These operations will record your creative results. It is recommended to save the current practice draft before starting subsequent creation.

FAQ

There is no sound when pressing the keys, what should I check first?

Please first check the phone system volume and current audio output device, and confirm that you are not stuck in an error message or export pop-up window. There is no need to connect to a Mac to produce local sounds on your phone; if you choose external MIDI output, please check the local volume and the receiving channel of the external host software respectively.

Is it another song after switching to other desktops?

No. The four desktops of the synthesizer control the same music project from different dimensions. Switching desktops does not create a new project, and there is no need to import sound materials repeatedly.

Can it be used directly as an instrument plug-in on Mac?

It can send MIDI control commands to the Mac, but the local sound source of the phone and the internal instruments of the Mac software belong to two independent channels. When you need your computer to produce sound, first connect your Mac, select a MIDI target, and configure the input mapping in the host DAW.
