Use mobile phones to assist editing: from browsing the timeline to splitting and undoing

Timeline scaling, frame-by-frame movement and common editing operation guide · Read about 6 minutes

When editing videos, frequently moving back and forth between the keyboard, mouse, and timeline can easily interrupt the creative rhythm. Through NinoPad's "Application Creation Station", you can place your phone next to the keyboard and complete timeline zooming, notch positioning and playback control with one hand.

Whether you are using professional DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro, or daily light editing, NinoPad can provide quick control for front-end software.

Practice Tips:First use the practice project to familiarize yourself with frame-by-frame browsing and playback, and then try operations that modify clips. The controls available may vary between software and workspaces.

1. First enter the application creation platform and confirm the current editing software.

When you open a supported editing software on your Mac and enter the "Application Creation Station", the system will automatically recognize the software environment and display the corresponding operations. Taking video clipping as an example, the commonly supported operations include:

  • Timeline browsing:Timeline zoom, fit to timeline, previous notch and next notch;
  • Alignment:Frame-by-frame fine-tuning (previous frame/next frame) and notch positioning;
  • Cut control:Split segments, delete selections, undo and redo;
  • Playback control and marking:Play/pause switching and adding key markers.

Two and three steps of safe practical exercises

In order to familiarize yourself with the feel without affecting the ongoing formal project, it is recommended to create a new test project containing a short test fragment and perform the following three-step exercise:

  1. Step 1: Select the test timeline segment
    Open the test timeline on your Mac and make sure the clipping window has focus.
  2. Step 2: Small frame-by-frame movement and notch positioning
    Tap the "Previous Frame" or "Next Frame" button on your phone and observe whether the Mac playhead aligns with the screen jump point.
  3. Step 3: Test playback, splitting and undoing
    Press the "Play/Pause" preview screen on your phone, tap "Split" to cut the test clip, then press the "Undo" button to restore, and confirm that the shortcut keys respond normally.

3. Color adjustment and multi-parameter fine-tuning

After switching the workspace, first look at the parameter names and available status displayed on the phone. Whether you can adjust a value depends on the capabilities of your current software; make small changes to one first and confirm the results on your Mac before continuing with other parameters. Whether the revocation takes effect also depends on the actual behavior of the software.

4. Horizontal screen holding and division of labor between hands

Place the mobile phone horizontally on the left side of the keyboard, and use your left thumb to blindly operate playback, pause, cut, and undo, and your right hand to focus on operating the mouse or tablet, forming an efficient two-hand division of labor.
