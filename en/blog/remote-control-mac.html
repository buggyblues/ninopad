Control Your Mac from iPhone or iPad

LAN code scanning and pairing, virtual trackpad gestures and dual-mode keyboard operation · Read about 6 minutes

Whether you are remotely controlling audio and video playback on the sofa, moving around in a conference room to control a presentation computer, or turning your iPhone or iPad into a portable touch secondary screen for your Mac, NinoPad can provide you with a brisk and smooth remote control experience.

Based on the direct point-to-point connection of the local LAN, you can monitor the Mac desktop, control the cursor, scroll smoothly with two fingers on your mobile device by just scanning the QR code, and supports draft editing and real-time key input.

Let’s do a little exercise first:After connecting to your Mac, try cursor movement and text input in a blank document. When you need to view the computer screen, turn on the screen preview.

1. LAN screen monitoring and point-to-point transmission

First run NinoPad on both ends, connect to a mutually accessible LAN and complete pairing. Moving the cursor and entering text require Mac accessibility permissions; viewing the desktop screen also requires screen recording permissions. After connecting, first open a test window on your Mac, and then see if the phone preview corresponds to this window.

2. Two display modes: control while watching and full-screen viewing

For different operation scenarios, NinoPad provides flexible viewports and operation layouts:

1. Watch and control on the desktop

The upper half of the phone screen uses a small window to monitor the focus area of the Mac desktop in real time, while the lower half is concentrated on a large virtual touchpad, stepless scroll bars and commonly used shortcut actions. It is especially suitable for holding the phone with one hand, monitoring the rendering or compilation progress, and blindly operating the cursor and scroll wheel.

2. Expand full screen view

When a larger preview is required, use the full-screen entry in the screen preview area. After expanding, make sure you see the target window; after returning to the desktop, continue using the trackpad, scrolling, and common keys. On-screen feedback still needs to correspond to the actual window on your Mac.

3. Virtual touchpad: intuitive gesture experience

NinoPad brings the core interaction logic of Apple Magic Trackpad to the touch screen:

  • One-finger swipe:Smoothly position the cursor and support adaptive acceleration curve;
  • One-finger tap/two-finger tap:A single finger tap corresponds to a left mouse click, and a two-finger tap directly brings up the contextual right-click menu;
  • Swipe up and down with two fingers:Two-finger smooth scrolling with natural inertia damping makes browsing long web pages and codes easy and smooth;
  • Double click and long press and drag:Easily drag the window title bar, select a text area, or move timeline elements with one hand.

4. Dual-mode keyboard architecture: draft pre-editing vs real-time terminal input

For different input scenarios, NinoPad provides two distinct keyboard input modes:

  • Text mode: Suitable for writing long texts, replying to messages, and organizing meeting shorthand. Make full use of the input method and speech-to-text tools on the mobile phone. After repeatedly checking and confirming that it is correct, click "Up Screen" and the entire text will be accurately delivered to the current focus cursor on the Mac at once.
  • Real time mode: Specially optimized for terminal command line, code debugging and shortcut key combinations. Each key input (e.g. Ctrl+C, Cmd+W, Tab, Esc, arrow keys and Enter) are all sent to the current input position of the Mac on the fly.

Before you connect

Can I control my Mac over the internet?

NinoPad controls a paired Mac over the same local network. It does not provide internet remote desktop or a cloud relay. Both devices need a reachable local Wi-Fi network; guest-network isolation can prevent pairing.

Which devices and systems do I need?

Use iOS / iPadOS 17 or later and the free Mac companion on macOS 13 or later, with Apple Silicon or Intel. Input control requires Accessibility permission; screen mirroring also requires Screen Recording permission.

What is free, and what does Pro unlock?

Basic keypad and system controls are free. Pro unlocks advanced decks, custom dynamic wallpapers and standalone instruments. Weekly, annual and lifetime plans provide the same Pro features; see the App Store checkout for local prices.
