How to Control Your Mac from an iPhone or iPad: Setup, Pairing, and Troubleshooting

Updated 2026-10-05 · 11 min read

Whether you want to pause media from across the room, advance slides during a presentation, or simply keep a dedicated trackpad and shortcut deck beside your computer, your iPhone or iPad can serve as a natural extension of your Mac.

NinoPad turns your iOS or iPadOS device into a wireless trackpad, keyboard, and screen viewer over your local network. This guide walks you through downloading the companion app, pairing over Wi-Fi, and confirming your first inputs, followed by a step-by-step troubleshooting checklist. Keep in mind that NinoPad operates over your local area network—it does not provide internet-based remote desktop access or cloud relay services.

Clarify your goals: Which Mac controls do you need?

Before adjusting settings, take a moment to identify how you plan to use NinoPad. Moving the cursor and triggering key combinations are input controls, whereas seeing your Mac desktop on a mobile device is screen mirroring. macOS handles these permissions separately, so if you only need a trackpad, you never have to grant Screen Recording access.

If you simply want extra controls next to your keyboard, start by verifying cursor movement and clicks. You can always enable screen mirroring or build personalized shortcut layouts later once the basic connection is running smoothly.

Keep in mind that connecting to the same Wi-Fi network name does not guarantee that devices can see one another. Public hotspots, university networks, corporate environments, and router guest modes often enable client isolation, which prevents devices from discovering each other. If pairing fails, check your network environment before repeatedly scanning the same QR code.

NinoPad consists of two distinct components: the mobile app and the desktop companion. The Mac companion is distributed through the official GitHub Releases page rather than the Mac App Store. Make sure you download both components from their respective official sources.

Four steps to connect: Install, pair, and test

  1. Install and launch NinoPad from the App Store on your iPhone or iPad. On your Mac, visit the official download page on GitHub Releases, grab the latest .dmg installer, drag NinoPad into your Applications folder, and launch it.
  2. Click the NinoPad cat icon in your Mac menu bar to bring up the pairing QR code. Ensure your Mac companion is running and that both devices are connected to the same local Wi-Fi.
  3. Open NinoPad on your mobile device, head to the Devices tab, and tap the QR code connection option. Point your camera at the Mac screen. Alternatively, if your Mac appears in the nearby devices list, tap its name and enter the 6-digit pairing code shown on your Mac.
  4. Follow the system prompts on your Mac to grant the necessary permissions, then verify the connection status on your phone. Open a blank document on your Mac, glide your finger across the mobile screen, and tap to confirm that cursor inputs register correctly on your computer.

Running this quick input test right away gives you a clear baseline. If the cursor moves, your local connection and Accessibility permissions are working properly. If the screen preview remains blank afterward, you can focus specifically on Screen Recording permissions without having to repeat the initial pairing setup.

Always use official distribution channels: the App Store for iOS/iPadOS and the official GitHub Releases repository for macOS. Quick links are provided at the end of this guide, and you can find further permission details in our companion configuration guide.

Permissions guide: Managing input and screen recording

On your Mac, open System Settings → Privacy & Security to verify NinoPad's authorizations. Depending on your macOS version, the screen capture permission may appear as “Screen Recording” or “Screen & System Audio Recording.” Follow the labels in your operating system alongside the companion's on-screen prompts.

PermissionFunctionWhen required
Mac AccessibilityEnables cursor movement, mouse clicks, and keyboard shortcut deliveryRequired whenever controlling Mac input
Mac Screen RecordingAllows desktop display capture to be streamed to your mobile deviceRequired only for screen mirroring; not needed for trackpad or keyboard input alone
iPhone / iPad Local NetworkAllows the mobile app to discover and connect to your Mac over Wi-FiAllow it during setup; check NinoPad in iOS Settings if previously denied
iPhone / iPad CameraScans the pairing QR code displayed on your MacPrompted when using the QR code pairing method

If the Mac companion prompts you to quit and reopen after granting permissions, do so to apply the new settings. If you move the application bundle or update the app later, make sure System Settings reflects the active binary rather than an older, inactive copy. Never disable your firewall or disable core system security protections to troubleshoot connectivity.

Hands-on: Trackpad gestures, typing, and mirroring

Using your iPhone as a trackpad

Select a workspace that features a trackpad surface. Move the pointer across the screen with a single finger, tap to click, and slide two fingers up and down to scroll through long documents or web pages. Watch the Mac display to confirm your movements. When multiple windows are open, click the intended window first to bring it to the foreground before performing further actions.

Sending text and shortcuts reliably

Before sending large amounts of text, open a blank document on your Mac, position your cursor inside, and send a short test phrase from your phone. Make sure your input lands where expected before sending longer text blocks or triggering key combinations. Shortcuts apply directly to whichever Mac app currently has focus, and the same combination can trigger different commands across different apps. Start with a familiar, reversible shortcut to verify behavior safely.

Once basic keystrokes respond consistently, you can bind your most frequent key combinations to custom buttons. We recommend starting with two or three essential everyday actions, confirming their responsiveness on your Mac, and then expanding your custom panel. Refer to the custom keypad guide linked below for step-by-step layout advice.

Enabling screen mirroring when needed

Once Screen Recording permission is active on your Mac, open the screen preview in NinoPad and switch to full-screen view if you need a larger display. Bring up a distinct, recognizable window on your Mac to confirm that the mobile preview updates in real time. Remember that the screen preview and input response need to be checked separately; if one works while the other fails, focus solely on the troubleshooting steps for that specific feature.

Streaming quality depends on local network bandwidth, system workload, and display content. This guide does not guarantee specific latency numbers or frame rates, nor does it support connections outside your local network. If your primary goal is clicking and triggering shortcuts, confirm that input works reliably before keeping an active screen stream open.

When reaching out to technical support, include the operating system versions on both devices, the NinoPad app versions, your connection method, and the exact step that failed: discovery, pairing, input, or display mirroring. For your security, never share pairing codes, sensitive documents, or unredacted screen captures publicly. A network timeout alone does not prove a faulty router, firewall, or gateway; sharing precise symptoms is far more helpful than guessing the underlying cause.

What is free, and when do you need Pro?

The basic keypad, core system controls, and the Mac companion app are completely free to use. We encourage you to pair your devices and verify mouse and keyboard responsiveness before considering an upgrade. Pro unlocks specialized professional workspaces, custom dynamic wallpapers, and standalone musical instruments; individual feature availability is indicated by the in-app unlock prompts.

Weekly, annual and lifetime options provide the same Pro feature access. Your App Store purchase confirmation shows the applicable local price and terms. Paying for Pro is not a fix for pairing or permission problems.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use mobile data to control my Mac at home while I am away?

No. The NinoPad Mac control features described in this guide require both devices to be reachable on the same local area network. The app does not provide public remote desktop routing or cloud-based relay infrastructure. Having the software installed on both devices will not bridge a mobile data connection to a remote home Mac.

Can the Mac use Ethernet while my iPhone uses Wi-Fi?

Yes, provided both connections reside on the same local network where inter-device traffic is permitted. However, router or administrator policies can isolate wired and wireless network segments. For your initial setup, we recommend connecting both devices to the same standard Wi-Fi network to verify communication.

Do I need Screen Recording just to use my iPhone as a trackpad?

No. Moving the cursor, clicking, and sending keystrokes only require macOS Accessibility permission. Screen Recording is solely used to stream your Mac desktop back to your mobile screen. You can use input controls without ever turning on screen capture.

Does the iPad replace the Mac companion or run Mac apps itself?

No. The mobile workspaces function as remote control interfaces that send commands to a paired Mac and display status updates; Mac software continues to run natively on the Mac hardware. Built-in offline tools like the local calculator or standalone instruments operate locally on iOS, but their offline capability does not extend to Mac control workspaces.
